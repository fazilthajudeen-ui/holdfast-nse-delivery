The blind spot

About half of every trade
never leaves the day.

Every evening NSE reports how many traded shares were actually delivered to buyers. The rest were bought and sold within the same day. On a price chart, both look exactly the same.

How it works

One score. Four honest steps.

Each stock is compared only with its own past — never with a fixed rule. Only data from before each day is used.

01

How much was delivered?

Out of all shares traded today, how many did buyers actually take home? That is the stock’s delivery %.

02

What is usual for this stock?

Some stocks always have high delivery, some always low. So we find each stock’s usual level — the middle of its last 60 days.

03

Is it above or below usual?

We measure the gap between recent delivery and usual, averaged over five days so one odd day doesn’t fool us.

04

One clear verdict.

The gap is ranked against the stock’s own last year into a score from 0 to 100. 70+ means Strong: buyers are holding. Under 30 means Weak: mostly day-trading.

What we don’t claim

  • Delivery doesn’t tell you which stock will rise next. It tells you how real a move that already happened looks.
  • One year of data is a pilot. The 20-day gap is suggestive, not proven (p above 0.05).
  • Our breakout-prediction model failed its pre-registered test. We publish that too.
